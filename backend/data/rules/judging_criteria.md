# UFC Judging Criteria — The 10-Point Must System

## Overview

UFC and most sanctioned MMA competitions use the **10-Point Must System**, originally adopted from boxing and adapted for the multi-disciplinary nature of mixed martial arts. Under this system:

- The winner of each round receives **10 points**
- The loser of each round receives **9 points** (in a competitive round)
- **10-8 rounds** are awarded when one fighter dominates, scores a knockdown, or achieves complete control
- **10-7 rounds** are extremely rare and reserved for exceptionally dominant performances

## Official Judging Criteria (In Priority Order)

The **Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts** specify that judges must evaluate rounds using the following criteria, **in order of priority**:

### 1. Effective Striking

The primary criterion. Judges evaluate which fighter is landing the most effective strikes — those that cause visible damage, disrupt the opponent, or advance toward a finish.

- **Quality over quantity**: A single knockdown scores more than 10 jabs
- **Legal target areas**: Head, body, legs — not the back of the head or spine
- **Knockdowns**: A fighter touching the canvas from a strike results in an automatic 10-8 round consideration
- **Accumulation**: Consistent clean shots that redden skin, cause swelling, or affect movement

### 2. Effective Grappling

The second-most important criterion. Judges assess:

- **Takedowns**: A clean double-leg takedown scores, but only if the fighter **does something with it** (advances position, strikes, threatens submission)
- **Reversals and sweeps**: Getting off the bottom or reversing position
- **Submission attempts**: Near-finishes with chokes or joint locks
- **Cage control/pinning**: Trapping the opponent against the fence with meaningful impact

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Takedowns that are immediately stood back up, or takedowns followed by complete inactivity, score VERY LITTLE. Judges are instructed to weigh "effective" grappling — action that does damage or threatens a finish.

### 3. Aggression

The **third** criterion (NOT the primary one, though often misapplied):

- Moving forward and pressing the action
- Attempting to finish rather than stalling or holding
- **Tiebreaker only**: Aggression is used to break ties between fighters who are otherwise equal on the first two criteria

> [!WARNING]
> Aggression is frequently over-weighted by judges, leading to controversial decisions where the more active but less accurate/effective fighter wins. This has been a subject of ongoing reform discussions.

### 4. Octagon/Ring Control

The final tiebreaker:

- Controlling where the fight takes place (keeping it standing or forcing it to the ground)
- Controlling pace and tempo
- Making the opponent react rather than dictating action

## How Rounds Are Scored

### 10-9 Round (Close Competitive Round)
The round winner scores 10, loser scores 9. Used when:
- One fighter lands more effective strikes/grappling
- Neither fighter was badly hurt or dominated

### 10-8 Round (Dominant Round or Knockdown)
Used when:
- One fighter scores a knockdown (or near-finish via strikes)
- One fighter completely dominates for the full 5 minutes
- One fighter is badly hurt but survives the round

### 10-7 Round (Exceptional Dominance)
Reserved for near-miraculous situations — essentially, a fighter so dominated that only survival makes it not a stoppage.

## Common Judging Controversies

### The "Last Round Bias"
Research has shown judges tend to over-weight the final round. A fighter who dominated rounds 1-4 but had a rough round 5 sometimes loses on scorecards that shouldn't reflect such a close fight.

### Takedown Inflation
Some judges score takedowns without evaluating what the wrestler does after securing the takedown. A passive top position with no strikes or submission attempts should score minimally.

### Aggression Misapplication
Charging forward and throwing without landing is not aggression in the judging sense. Aggression should mean *meaningful forward pressure that advances the objective of finishing the fight*.

### High Guard Deception
Fighters who adopt a high guard can make landed strikes appear blocked. Some judges score blocked shots; others correctly assess actual impact. This inconsistency causes controversy.

## The Role of Athletic Commission Judges

UFC events in the United States are sanctioned by state Athletic Commissions (Nevada, California, New York, Texas, etc.). Each commission provides judges. The UFC has no control over which judges are assigned, though they can provide feedback to commissions.

Judges are required to:
- Watch the fight from cageside
- Score each round independently immediately after it ends
- Submit written scorecards at the conclusion of each round

## Efforts Toward Reform

The MMA community has advocated for several improvements:

1. **Fighter/coach feedback loops** with athletic commissions
2. **Video review** of judging decisions after events
3. **Open-score rounds** — announcing the score after each round so fighters/coaches know where they stand (used in some promotions)
4. **Hiring more experienced/specialized MMA judges** rather than repurposing boxing judges
5. **More aggressive application of 10-8 rounds** for dominant performances
