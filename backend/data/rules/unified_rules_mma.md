# Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts

## Overview

The Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts were created by the New Jersey State Athletic Control Board (NJSACB) in 2000 and adopted by Nevada's athletic commission in 2001. They have since become the standard regulatory framework for MMA competitions sanctioned by athletic commissions throughout North America and internationally.

The rules were developed collaboratively between state athletic commissions, the UFC, and MMA organizations to provide a consistent, safe, and fair environment for competition.

## Weight Classes

MMA competitions are organized by weight class. Fighters must make weight within the prescribed limits:

| Class | Limit |
|---|---|
| Strawweight | 115 lbs |
| Flyweight | 125 lbs |
| Bantamweight | 135 lbs |
| Featherweight | 145 lbs |
| Lightweight | 155 lbs |
| Welterweight | 170 lbs |
| Middleweight | 185 lbs |
| Light Heavyweight | 205 lbs |
| Heavyweight | 265 lbs (no upper limit officially) |
| Super Heavyweight | Over 265 lbs |

Women's divisions: Strawweight (115), Flyweight (125), Bantamweight (135), Featherweight (145).

## Permitted Techniques

### Striking
The following striking techniques are permitted:

**On the feet (standing position)**:
- Punches to the head and body
- Kicks to the head and body
- Knee strikes to the head and body
- Elbows to the head and body
- Foot stomps to the foot

**On the ground (grounded opponent)**:
- Punches to the head and body
- Elbows to the head and body
- Knees to the body (not to the head of a grounded opponent)
- Kicks to the body (not to the head of a grounded opponent)

### Grappling
- Clinching, holding, and controlling opponents
- Takedowns and throws
- Submission holds (chokes, joint locks)
- All grappling techniques not specifically prohibited

## Prohibited Actions (Fouls)

The following actions constitute fouls and may result in point deductions or disqualification:

### Head and Neck
1. **Headbutts** — intentional or unintentional headbutts
2. **Eye gouging** of any kind
3. **Biting** or spitting at an opponent
4. **Throat strikes** of any kind, including grabbing the trachea
5. **Clawing, pinching, twisting the flesh**

### Striking Fouls
6. **Strikes to the back of the head or spine** (the "rabbit punch")
7. **Downward pointing elbow strikes** (12-6 elbows — elbow from directly above)
8. **Groin attacks** of any kind
9. **Knee strikes to the head of a grounded opponent**
10. **Stomping to the head of a grounded opponent**
11. **Kicks to the head of a grounded opponent**
12. **Soccer kicks** to the head

### Grappling Fouls
13. **Small joint manipulation** (fingers and toes) — only larger joints allowed
14. **Hair pulling**
15. **Grabbing the fence or ropes** with fingers or toes
16. **Holding the opponent's shorts or gloves**

### Conduct Fouls
17. **Spitting at the opponent**
18. **Intentionally placing a finger in any orifice or cut**
19. **Timidity** — avoiding contact, faking injury, stalling
20. **Interference by the corner**
21. **Throwing an opponent outside the ring or cage**
22. **Flagrantly disregarding the referee's instructions**
23. **Unsportsmanlike conduct** causing injury

## Grounded Opponent Definition

A fighter is considered "grounded" when any part of the body other than the soles of the feet is touching the canvas. This includes a single hand, knee, or any other body part touching the floor. This definition is critical because it determines which strikes are legal against that opponent.

Some athletic commissions have updated this definition: in certain jurisdictions, a fighter on both hands and one knee is not considered grounded (only hands + knees simultaneously or a single touch from a body part other than hands/feet counts as grounded).

## Judging Criteria

Bouts are scored on a 10-point must system. Judges score each round on the following criteria, in order of priority:

1. **Effective striking** — The legal strikes that have the greatest effect
2. **Effective grappling** — Takedowns, reversals, submission attempts
3. **Aggression** — The fighter who consistently moves forward
4. **Octagon control** — Controlling pace and position of the fight

A round winner receives 10 points. The loser receives 9 points (or 8 for a dominant round, or 7 for rare circumstances). A knockdown typically results in a 10-8 round.

## Round Structure

- **Non-championship fights**: 3 rounds × 5 minutes each
- **Championship fights**: 5 rounds × 5 minutes each
- **Between rounds**: 1 minute rest period

## Ways to Win

1. **KO (Knockout)** — Opponent is rendered unconscious by legal strikes
2. **TKO (Technical Knockout)** — Referee stops the fight because the opponent cannot defend themselves intelligently; or doctor stoppage; or corner stoppage
3. **Submission** — Opponent taps the mat or opponent verbally submits or is rendered unconscious via submission hold
4. **Unanimous Decision** — All three judges score in favor of the same fighter
5. **Split Decision** — Two judges favor one fighter, one judge favors the other
6. **Majority Decision** — Two judges favor one fighter, one judge scores it a draw
7. **Technical Draw** — An accidental foul causes a cut or injury that prevents continuation before enough rounds for a decision
8. **No Contest** — An accidental foul occurs in the early rounds (varies by jurisdiction — typically before round 3 of a 3-round fight or before round 3 of a 5-round fight)
9. **Disqualification** — Fighter commits a flagrant foul after warnings

## Equipment and Attire

- **Gloves**: 4-ounce MMA gloves (open-finger, allowing grappling)
- **Mouthguard**: Required
- **Groin protector**: Required for male fighters
- **Shorts/Trunks**: Approved by the commission
- No shoes, no shirts in competition

## Corner and Second Requirements

Each fighter is allowed up to three corner people. Corner personnel must:
- Be licensed by the athletic commission
- Stay outside the fence/cage during competition
- Only enter the octagon during the rest periods between rounds and after the fight ends
- Not interfere with the bout in any way

Corner can throw in the towel (surrender) to stop the fight on behalf of their fighter at any time.
