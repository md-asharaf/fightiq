# Amanda Nunes — The Greatest Women's MMA Fighter of All Time

## Personal Information

- **Full Name**: Amanda Lourenço Nunes
- **Nickname**: The Lioness
- **Born**: May 30, 1988, Pojuca, Bahia, Brazil
- **Nationality**: Brazilian-American
- **Height**: 5 ft 8 in (173 cm)
- **Reach**: 69 inches (175 cm)
- **Fighting Style**: Boxing, Muay Thai, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

## Fighting Record

As of her retirement in 2024, Amanda Nunes has an MMA record of 23 wins and 5 losses.

- **Wins**: 13 by knockout/TKO, 3 by submission, 7 by decision
- **Losses**: 5 (all before her championship run)

## Championship History

Amanda Nunes is the greatest women's MMA fighter of all time, having held UFC championships in two weight classes simultaneously:

1. **UFC Women's Bantamweight Championship (135 lbs)**: Won on July 9, 2016 at UFC 200, defeating Miesha Tate by rear-naked choke in round 1. Defended the title seven times.

2. **UFC Women's Featherweight Championship (145 lbs)**: Won on December 29, 2018 at UFC 232, defeating Cris Cyborg by TKO in round 1 to become the first woman ever to hold UFC titles in two divisions simultaneously.

## Notable Fights

- **UFC 200 (2016)**: Defeated Miesha Tate (then-champion) by RNC in round 1 to claim her first UFC title.
- **UFC 207 (2016)**: Destroyed Ronda Rousey by TKO in 48 seconds — one of the most dominant title-fight performances in UFC history.
- **UFC 215 (2017)**: Defeated Valentina Shevchenko by split decision in their first meeting — a highly competitive fight.
- **UFC 232 (2018)**: Knocked out Cris Cyborg — the most feared women's fighter in the world — in round 1, ending Cyborg's 13-year unbeaten streak in MMA.
- **UFC 239 (2019)**: Submitted Holly Holm by guillotine choke in round 1.
- **UFC 250 (2020)**: Defeated Felicia Spencer by unanimous decision.
- **UFC 259 (2021)**: Defeated Megan Anderson by arm-triangle choke in round 1.
- **UFC 277 (2022)**: Won a razor-close split decision rematch against Julianna Peña — avenging her shocking upset loss at UFC 269.
- **UFC 289 (2023)**: Defeated Irene Aldana by unanimous decision in her final UFC title defense.

## The Ronda Rousey Destruction

At UFC 207, Nunes destroyed Ronda Rousey — the most famous woman in combat sports — in 48 seconds with a combination of straight punches that Rousey could not handle. Rousey had been the biggest name in women's MMA and had sold out arenas worldwide. Nunes' demolition of Rousey announced her as the undisputed queen of women's MMA.

## The Cyborg Upset

At UFC 232, Nunes knocked out Cristiane "Cyborg" Justino in 51 seconds. Cyborg had gone 13 years without a loss across multiple promotions and was considered almost unstoppable due to her size advantage and ferocious pressure. Nunes absorbed Cyborg's early pressure and countered with a combination that put Cyborg down for the first time in her career, completing the greatest upset in women's MMA history.

## Fighting Style & Attributes

- **Devastating Punching Power**: Known as the hardest puncher in women's MMA history, with KO power that has finished multiple champions.
- **Precision Boxing**: Technical combinations with elite timing and accuracy.
- **Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu**: Black belt in BJJ with strong submission game, trained at American Top Team.
- **Durability**: Extraordinary ability to take shots and continue moving forward.
- **Championship Experience**: Has performed at the highest level for nearly a decade — a rarity in any combat sport.

## Training at American Top Team

Nunes trains at American Top Team (ATT) in Coconut Creek, Florida, one of the premier MMA gyms in the world. She trains alongside world-class talent including Jorge Masvidal, Dustin Poirier, and many others. Her husband, Nina Ansaroff, is also a professional MMA fighter.

## Legacy

Amanda Nunes is universally recognized as the greatest women's MMA fighter of all time. She defeated virtually every significant name in women's MMA: Ronda Rousey, Cris Cyborg, Miesha Tate, Holly Holm, Valentina Shevchenko (twice), Germaine de Randamie, and more. No other fighter in the history of women's MMA has achieved what Nunes has.
