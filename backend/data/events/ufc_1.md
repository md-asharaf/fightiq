# UFC 1: The Beginning — November 12, 1993

## Event Information

- **Event**: UFC 1: The Beginning
- **Date**: November 12, 1993
- **Venue**: McNichols Sports Arena, Denver, Colorado, USA
- **Attendance**: 7,800
- **PPV Buys**: Approximately 86,592
- **Commentator**: Bill Wallace, Brian Kilmeade
- **Promoter**: Semaphore Entertainment Group (SEG)

## Background and Concept

UFC 1 was created by Art Davie, Rorion Gracie, and Bob Meyrowitz with the explicit goal of answering one question: "Which martial art is most effective in a real fight?" The event was designed with almost no rules — to determine supremacy between different fighting disciplines.

Original concept rules:
- No time limits
- No judges
- No weight classes
- Only biting and eye-gouging were prohibited
- Victory by submission, KO, or corner stoppage

Eight fighters representing different disciplines competed in a single-elimination bracket.

## Tournament Bracket

### Participants

1. **Royce Gracie** — Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (Gracie family)
2. **Gerard Gordeau** — Savate (French kickboxing)
3. **Ken Shamrock** — Catch Wrestling (Pancrase)
4. **Patrick Smith** — Taekwondo/Kickboxing
5. **Teila Tuli** (Tila Tequila's connection unrelated) — Sumo
6. **Kevin Rosier** — Kickboxing
7. **Jason DeLucia** — Shoot Wrestling
8. **Zane Frazier** — Karate

### Quarterfinals (Round 1)

**Match 1**: Gerard Gordeau def. Teila Tuli by TKO (0:26 of Round 1)
- Gordeau knocked out two of Tuli's teeth with a kick.

**Match 2**: Kevin Rosier def. Zane Frazier by TKO (4:20)

**Match 3**: Royce Gracie def. Art Jimmerson by submission (rear-naked choke) (0:25)
- Jimmerson (a professional boxer) showed up wearing a single boxing glove.

**Match 4**: Ken Shamrock def. Patrick Smith by submission (heel hook) (1:49)

### Semifinals

**Match 5**: Gerard Gordeau def. Kevin Rosier by TKO

**Match 6**: Royce Gracie def. Ken Shamrock by rear-naked choke (0:57)
- This would begin one of MMA's great rivalries.

### Final

**Tournament Winner**: Royce Gracie def. Gerard Gordeau by rear-naked choke (1:44)

## Royce Gracie's Significance

Royce Gracie's victory at UFC 1 — and the subsequent UFC events — is considered one of the most significant moments in martial arts history. As the smallest fighter in the tournament, defeating wrestlers, kickboxers, and boxers with Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu submissions demonstrated that technique and leverage (BJJ principles) could overcome size and striking power.

Rorion Gracie had invited the fighters specifically so that his family's martial art could prove its dominance. The strategy succeeded beyond all expectations and sparked a global revolution in martial arts training.

## Impact on Martial Arts

The televised proof that a relatively small grappler using Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu could defeat much larger opponents from other disciplines:
- Caused an explosion of BJJ gym enrollments worldwide
- Permanently changed how all martial arts train — leading to cross-training becoming standard
- Created the concept of MMA as we know it today
- Led to the development of the "complete fighter" — someone proficient in both striking and grappling

## The Gracie Family

The Gracie family of Brazil developed Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu from Mitsuyo Maeda's Judo in the early 20th century. The family had a tradition of issuing "Gracie Challenges" — open invitations to fighters of any discipline to fight a Gracie in a no-rules match. These challenges, and the Gracies' consistent success, led directly to the creation of the UFC as a formal showcase.

## Historical Legacy

UFC 1 is the founding document of mixed martial arts as a global sport. The event that started with 7,800 spectators in Denver would eventually grow into a multi-billion dollar organization watched by millions worldwide. Every modern MMA gym, every UFC event, every fighter who cross-trains traces their lineage back to the question asked that night in Denver.
